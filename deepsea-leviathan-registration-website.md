# DEEPSEA LEVIATHAN

**Registration Website — Content & Design Brief**

**30 November 2026  •  RIT Evolute**

## 1. Purpose of the Website

This document tells the website team what the Deepsea Leviathan registration site must contain, how it should look and feel, and the exact text to use. The site has three jobs:

- Get teams registered.
- Give participants the key facts: date, venue, team size, event structure.
- Send every registered participant into the official WhatsApp participants group.

The site should make people curious, not informed. It hints at what the game is through its theme. It never explains what happens at the stations.

## 2. About the Game (Internal — Do Not Publish As-Is)

Deepsea Leviathan is a team event. Teams move through stations. Clearing a station earns the team energy. Teams need enough energy to face and defeat the Leviathan at the end.

*This section is context for the website team only. The site must express this idea through visuals and mood, not through explanation. Never write "complete stations to earn energy" on the site.*

## 4. Theme & Visual Direction

Core idea: the visitor is descending into the deep ocean. The deeper they scroll, the closer they get to something enormous. Along the way, something is quietly charging up.

### 4.1 The Descent (scroll = depth)

- Background shifts from surface blue at the top to near-black abyss at the bottom.
- A thin depth meter runs along the side of the screen. It works as the scroll indicator (0 m at the top, around 11,000 m at the footer).
- Light rays fade out as you go deeper. Floating particles and bioluminescent specks appear lower down.
### 4.2 The Energy Hint (subtle — never labelled)

- A slim power bar or a row of 5 small cells (3 large + 2 smaller) glows near the depth meter.
- The cells light up one by one as the visitor scrolls through the page sections.
- Never label these as stations or energy. Let visitors connect the dots themselves.
- When all cells are lit (near the Register section), give a soft pulse — a feeling of being "ready".
### 4.3 The Leviathan's Presence

- Never show the full creature. Only hints: a huge shadow passing behind content, a single glowing eye, a fin edge at the bottom of the page.
- Sonar ping rings radiate occasionally from the CTA buttons.
- Optional: a faint low rumble sound, muted by default, with a toggle.
### 4.4 Palette

| Role | Colour | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface | Ocean blue | #1B6CA8 |
| Mid-depth | Deep navy | #0A2A43 |
| Abyss | Near-black | #02070D |
| Bioluminescence / energy | Cyan glow | #3FF5E6 |
| Leviathan / danger | Ember red | #E4572E |
| Text | Off-white | #E8F1F2 |

### 4.5 Typography & Motion

- Headings: a heavy, wide display font with a slightly worn/nautical feel.
- Body: a clean sans-serif, high readability on dark backgrounds.
- Data (date, depth meter, team size): monospaced, like an instrument readout.
- Motion should be slow and heavy, like water. No fast bouncy animations.
- Respect "prefers-reduced-motion": turn off parallax, shadow passes and particles.
- Keep text contrast at WCAG AA or better on all dark sections.

## 6. Do Not

- Do not explain what happens at any station.
- Do not mention energy, points or scoring in text. The theme hints at it; the words don't.
- Do not show the full Leviathan.
- Do not hide the date, venue, team size or WhatsApp link — these must be easy to find.

## 8. Technical Notes

- Mobile first — most visitors arrive from WhatsApp/Instagram on phones.
- Form can post to Google Sheets / Google Forms backend, or any simple database.
- Keep page weight low: compressed images, CSS/JS animations instead of video.
- Add Open Graph tags (title, tagline, dark ocean preview image) so shared links look good in WhatsApp.
